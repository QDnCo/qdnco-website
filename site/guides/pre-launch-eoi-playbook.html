The pre-launch and EOI campaign playbook

Last updated 2026-09-19 · 6 min read

A pre-launch campaign sells a decision to pay attention, not a flat. That distinction governs everything about how it should be built and how it should be judged, and getting it wrong produces a database of people who will not answer the phone at launch.

What you are actually selling

In pre-launch you are buying a qualified audience you can activate on launch day, not bookings. The campaign should be judged on how many of those people are still reachable and still interested when inventory opens — not on how many registrations it produced. That makes registration volume a vanity metric at this stage: ten thousand registrations that go cold within six weeks are worth less than four hundred that turn into site visits in the first fortnight, and the cheap campaign usually produces the first kind. Pre-launch collects interest, not commitment, so the number that predicts launch performance is the warm rate — the share of registrants still reachable and interested when inventory opens. Scarcity messaging only works because buyers believe it, so advertise only scarcity that is real. Judge the campaign on that warm rate, not on sign-ups.

This is why registration volume is a vanity metric at this stage. Ten thousand registrations that go cold in six weeks are worth less than four hundred that convert to site visits in the first fortnight, and the cheap version is almost always the first one.

What to promise, and what not to

Pre-launch is where compliance exposure is highest, because the project is least defined.

  • Safe: the location, the corridor, the developer's track record, the unit configurations under consideration, the stage the project is at, and an honest statement that pricing is indicative.
  • Dangerous: a firm price, a possession date, amenities not yet approved, and any scarcity claim you cannot evidence.
  • Non-negotiable: if the project is registrable and not yet registered, that determines what can be advertised at all. That is a question for the developer's counsel, before the campaign, not after.

"Founder allocation", "EOI stage", "only 20 units at this price" — these work precisely because buyers believe they are real. Used when they are not, they poison the launch they were meant to accelerate.

Measuring a phase with no bookings

  1. Cost per registration. The volume metric. Necessary, insufficient.
  2. Qualification rate at intake. The same five conditions. A pre-launch list that fails them is a list of names.
  3. Warm-rate at reactivation. Contact a sample two to three weeks after registration. What proportion still remember the project and still want the call? This is the number that predicts launch performance, and almost nobody measures it.
  4. Site-visit conversion at launch. The retrospective verdict on the whole phase.

The third one is the useful one and it costs almost nothing to run. A pre-launch list with a 15% warm rate and one with a 60% warm rate will behave completely differently on launch day, and you can know which you have three weeks before it matters rather than on the day.

Questions developers ask

What is an EOI campaign in real estate?

An expression-of-interest campaign collects registrations from prospective buyers before inventory is formally on sale, so there is a warm audience to activate at launch. It sells a decision to pay attention rather than a flat, which is why it should be judged on how many registrants are still reachable and interested at launch — not on registration volume.

Can you advertise a price during pre-launch?

Indicative pricing can usually be communicated if it is clearly labelled as indicative and subject to change. A firm price for inventory that is not yet sellable is the risky version. Where the project is registrable and not yet registered, what can be advertised at all is a legal question for the developer's counsel before the campaign starts.

How do you measure a pre-launch campaign with no sales?

Cost per registration, qualification rate at intake, and — the one that actually predicts launch performance — warm-rate at reactivation. Contact a sample two to three weeks after registration and measure what proportion still remember the project and still want the call. A 15% warm rate and a 60% warm rate behave completely differently on launch day.

Is scarcity marketing allowed in real estate advertising?

Scarcity claims are allowed when they are true and evidenced. "Only 20 units at this price" is fine if there are twenty units at that price. Used when it is not true it is both a compliance exposure under the accuracy requirements of the Act and a fast way to lose the trust of the exact audience you spent the pre-launch budget building.
