Budgets and unit economics

How long should I run a campaign before deciding it does not work?

Last updated 2026-09-19

Set the review window before launch using expected event volume, the project sales cycle and the decision being tested. A month may be a planning starting point, but no fixed duration or event count guarantees stable delivery or a valid conclusion. Check tracking and obvious mismatches immediately; assess qualified-lead and booking outcomes over cohorts with enough follow-up time.

Why early judgement is so costly

Restarting resets the learning. A campaign killed at day 10 and relaunched at day 20 has paid for the unstable phase twice and has no more information than it did at the start.

Compounding this, performance during learning is genuinely worse and genuinely unrepresentative, so the data most likely to make you kill a campaign is the data least worth acting on.

What you may act on early

Creative-level decisions, not campaign-level ones. If one concept out of five is clearly not producing after enough impressions, turning it off is a within-test decision and does not reset anything.

Structural changes — objective, audience, budget distribution — restart learning. Make them deliberately and rarely, and not on a Tuesday because the weekend was quiet.
