## What counts as a qualified lead?

A lead is qualified when it meets five written criteria: contactable within 48 hours across three attempts; budget inside the project price band; stated purchase intent within six months; located in a valid catchment for the project; and not already in the CRM within the previous 90 days. A lead that fails any one of the five is replaced free of charge if it is flagged within seven days.

## Why are the leads from my agency so bad?

Usually one of four things, in descending order of likelihood: nobody wrote down what "qualified" means, so volume was optimised instead; the campaign was optimised for form fills rather than for a downstream event; the targeting was broad because broad is cheaper per lead and the contract paid per lead; or the leads were fine and nobody called them inside 48 hours. The first and last are the most common, and they are the two nobody wants to check.

## How fast should a real estate lead be called?

Inside 48 hours, across at least three attempts, before a lead can fairly be assessed at all — that is the contactability standard written into our qualified-lead definition. Faster is materially better: a property enquiry is usually one of several submitted in the same browsing session, so the first firm to make contact is often the only one having a real conversation. Beyond about 72 hours, contact rates fall far enough that the lead is effectively a different, colder asset.

## How many leads does it take to book one unit?

Plan on 100 raw leads per booking until your own sales data proves better. The chain behind that number: roughly 60% of raw leads meet a reasonable qualification bar, and a strong sales team closes around 10% of those — which lands near six bookings per hundred qualified, or one booking per hundred raw leads once real-world slippage is included. It is deliberately the pessimistic case, because a budget built on the optimistic case has no room to be wrong.

## What does "sales-ready percentage" mean?

Sales-ready percentage is the share of delivered leads that your sales team, not the agency, judges worth pursuing after first contact. It is the multiplier that turns cost per lead into cost per useful lead. On a premium North Bangalore residential launch we delivered ₹266 cost per lead at 51% sales-ready — meaning the true cost of a lead worth working was roughly ₹520, and that second number is the one worth comparing.

## Meta ads or Google ads for a property launch?

Both, in different roles. Meta creates demand — it puts the project in front of people who were not searching for it, which is most of the market for a new launch, and it carries the volume. Google captures demand that already exists — someone typing a project name, a locality plus "apartments", or a competitor's name is far further down the funnel and converts at a much better rate on far less volume. Running Meta alone caps your quality; running Google alone caps your scale.

Source: https://qdnco.com/answers/meta-ads-vs-google-ads-real-estate.html

## How should Meta ads be structured for a real estate launch?

As a full funnel with four layers — top, middle, bottom and a separate retargeting pool — with channel-partner demand and end-buyer demand run as entirely separate campaigns. That separation is the structural decision that matters most: the two audiences respond to opposite creative, convert at different rates and, mixed together, will corrupt each other's optimisation signal so that neither performs.

## How should Google ads be set up for a real estate project?

In four layers: your own project and brand terms, locality plus category terms, category terms without a locality, and competitor terms where appropriate. Brand defence is the layer you cannot skip — it is the cheapest traffic you will ever buy and, if you leave it undefended, channel partners and aggregators will bid on your project name and resell you the lead you already paid to create.

## Should developers use AI-generated ad creatives?

Yes for the concepting, layout and volume that testing requires, and no for anything that depicts the actual property. AI generation makes it economic to run enough distinct concepts to find a winner and to refresh on fatigue rather than on budget cycles. But an AI-generated image of a building you are selling is a misrepresentation risk under RERA and a straightforward trust problem with buyers, so photography and approved renders remain the only honest source for the asset itself.

## What are the RERA rules for advertising a project?

Section 3(1) of the Real Estate (Regulation and Development) Act 2016 prohibits advertising, marketing, booking, selling or inviting persons to purchase any unit in a project that is not registered with the state authority. Once registered, the RERA registration number and the authority's website must appear on the advertisement, and what the advertisement depicts must match what has been approved and registered. This is a hard gate — we do not run advertising for an unregistered project.

Source: https://qdnco.com/answers/rera-advertising-rules.html

## Can I advertise before RERA registration comes through?

No. Section 3(1) bars advertising, marketing or inviting purchase for an unregistered project, and pre-launch EOI campaigns against an unregistered project fall inside that bar however they are worded. What you can legitimately do while registration is pending is everything that is not an invitation to purchase: the market read, the buyer research, the competitive scrape, the campaign architecture, the creative production and the CRM build.

## Is a marketing agency cheaper than a channel partner?

They are different instruments and the comparison only works per booking. A channel partner typically takes 1–3% of unit value and is paid on a closed sale, so the cost is high per unit but carries no upfront risk. Performance marketing costs money before anything sells but is far cheaper per booking once the funnel works. On a ₹1 crore unit, a 2% commission is ₹2,00,000; at a ₹400 cost per lead and 100 leads per booking, media is roughly ₹40,000 plus fee.

## What does a real estate lead funnel need to have?

Five things: capture that records the source, routing that assigns an owner within minutes, an alert that reaches a named human on their phone, a status model your sales team will actually update, and a feedback loop that sends qualification outcomes back to the ad platforms. ## What is generative engine optimisation (GEO)?

GEO is the practice of getting a business cited inside AI-generated answers — ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude, Perplexity, Google AI Overviews — rather than ranked in a list of blue links. The mechanics differ from SEO in one decisive way: an assistant lifts a passage and attributes it, so the unit of optimisation is a self-contained, specific, attributable statement rather than a page. ## Is marketing commercial or office space different from residential?

Substantially different in three ways. The audience is far smaller and defined by business need rather than demographics, so broad social targeting wastes most of its reach. The decision cycle is longer and involves more people, which makes retargeting and content far more important than a single conversion push. And search carries disproportionate weight, because commercial requirements are actively searched with specific terms — square footage, locality, floor plate — in a way homes usually are not.

## Do I need a landing page, or are lead forms enough?

Instant lead forms produce more leads at lower cost with worse contact rates; landing pages produce fewer, better and more expensive leads. Neither is correct in the abstract. If your sales team has spare capacity and you need volume to find the market, use instant forms with qualifying questions. If your team is saturated and wasting time on unreachable enquiries, a landing page filters before the lead exists, which is cheaper than filtering afterwards.

## When is the best time to launch a real estate campaign?

Work backwards: you need roughly six weeks of research, architecture and creative before going live, so the campaign starts when the launch is six weeks out. Beyond that, the two timing factors that matter are festival season, which lifts intent across Indian property markets and lifts auction prices with it, and the launch calendar of your own catchment. ## What is a good lead-to-site-visit ratio?

There is no reliable industry benchmark worth quoting, and anyone offering you one is guessing — it varies enormously with ticket size, distance and how the visit is scheduled. What matters is your own ratio, tracked from the first month, because it moves within weeks and it catches lead-quality and sales-process problems long before booking data would. Track it as cost per site visit rather than as a percentage, since that makes it comparable across campaigns.

## What is the difference between a lead and an enquiry?

In practice the two are used interchangeably and neither means anything on its own. Both describe a person who submitted their details, which tells you nothing about whether they can be reached, can afford the unit, are in your catchment, or already exist in your CRM. This is precisely why the useful term is "qualified lead" with a written definition attached, and why an agency quoting a price per lead without that definition is quoting a price for an undefined unit.
